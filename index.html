About

I am a field linguist working on Ngen (gnj), a Southern Mande language (< Mande < Niger-Congo) spoken by about one thousand people, mainly in the village of Djonkro, Prikro prefecture, Côte-d'Ivoire. I am writing a reference grammar of Ngen as my PhD project at the Department of Asian and African Studies, Humboldt-Universität zu Berlin, supervised by Prof. Tom Güldemann and funded by an Elsa-Neumann scholarship. I have been doing fieldwork in Côte d'Ivoire since 2019.

From September 2026 to December 2027 I hold an EFL Incoming Mobility Grant (Labex Empirical Foundations of Linguistics, Université Paris Cité) for a research stay at LACITO (CNRS), Paris.

What drives my research is the question of how language is constructed, and how it comes about that we understand each other at all. I also work on how AI tools can shorten the path from field recordings to an annotated corpus: speech recognition for an under-resourced language, speaker identification and semi-automatic glossing.

Projects

A Grammar of Ngen (PhD, 2024–2027)

A reference grammar of Ngen covering phonology and tone, nouns and noun phrases, pronominal elements, locative expressions, verbs and TAMP categories, non-verbal predication and clause structure. Data: elicitation and narratives recorded with several consultants in Djonkro, 2019–2025.

Documenting Ngen

About 100 hours of field recordings, segmented into a searchable database of transcribed, translated and glossed utterances, with tone marked and checked by ear. Published so far: a Ngen–English–French wordlist (~950 lexemes) and fieldwork samples in the Pangloss Collection.

AI-assisted processing of field data

Practical experiments in speeding up transcription with AI tools available to a single field linguist: a speech recognition model fine-tuned for Ngen (character error rate about 10%), voice-based speaker identification across the archive, and timed annotation sessions comparing work with and without machine drafts.
